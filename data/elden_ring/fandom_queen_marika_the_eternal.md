# Queen Marika the Eternal
Let a new epoch begin. An epoch glistening with life. Brandish the Elden Ring, for the Age of the Erdtree! - Queen Marika the Eternal — Melina
Queen Marika the Eternal is the central character in the world of Elden Ring. She is the divine ruler of the Lands Between and the current vessel of the Elden Ring, a god in truth. She grew the towering Erdtree, concealed the Realm of Shadow, and established the Golden Order. By the time of the game's events, she has long vanished from public view, though her influence persists through the Grace of Gold. Her Demigod offspring became shardbearers when they claimed Great Runes that dispersed when she shattered the Elden Ring.
Information about Marika is primarily provided by Melina, Finger Reader Enia, and various item descriptions.

## Characteristics

Queen Marika was a tall Numen woman with fair skin and long, flowing golden hair gathered into two uneven braids, the right being longer. She typically wore a circlet, gold armbands, gold serpentine bracelets, and either a black dress or a robe patterned with golden floral motifs. Often depicted in cruciform poses with her head bowed, she simultaneously evoked divinity, fragility, regality, and martyrdom.
In the present era, her body appears as a stone-like, petrified husk, fractured and cracked like broken porcelain. She is shackled and suspended on a great Rune Arc with a red needle-like shard piercing her abdomen like a spear. During the final confrontation, her body transforms into Radagon of the Golden Order before the Elden Beast reveals itself. Following the player's victory, she ultimately reverts to a fractured, statuesque form.

### Marika's Rune

Marika's Rune was the official seal of Queen Marika, used by her to control vassals, bestow grace, and seal away threats to her power.
Bearing a close resemblance to the Rune of Death, Marika's Rune is a Y-shaped Elden Rune with an upward crescent resembling the lower arc of the Elden Ring, believed to be the basin in which its blessing pool. Its shape parallels Marika's crucified form within the Erdtree. Variations of the Rune recur throughout the Lands Between, such as the Effigies of the Martyr, statues found in churches of Marika, and the numerous crosses found throughout Limgrave and Dominula, Windmill Village.

## Location

Queen Marika is found near the Fractured Marika site of grace on the Stone Platform within the Erdtree, accessible from Leyndell, Ashen Capital.

## Background

Marika came from the Shaman Village in the Scaduview. Her people had an affinity for trees and flesh that melded harmoniously with that of others. The hornsent tortured and sliced up shamans before stuffing them into the jars to be reborn as saints. The Two Fingers recognized Marika as an Empyrean and bestowed her a shadowbound beast in the form of her half-brother, Maliketh —a faithful follower, incapable of treachery. At some point during this period, Marika took the warrior Hoarah Loux as her consort, who later vowed to become Godfrey, her first Elden Lord. Through seduction and betrayal, she ascended to godhood at the Gate of Divinity atop the tower of Enir-Ilim, and became the vessel for the Elden Ring. This affair led to the birth of the Erdtree and Scadutree, and was considered a betrayal by the Hornsent. The Scadutree veiled the land of shadow upon its creation.
Eventually, Marika returned to the Shaman Village to find it abandoned, the people of her village were stolen away from their home, never to return. After cutting off a braid of her hair as an offering to the Grandmother, she bathed her home in the healing light of gold, knowing full well that there was no one to heal. She would never return again.

### Age of the Erdtree

In the early days, everything was in opposition to the Erdtree. With Godfrey’s aid, Marika waged a War Against the Giants atop the frigid northern mountains, where dwelled the Flame of Ruin capable of burning the Erdtree. Ultimately victorious, Marika was believed to have slain the Fell God of the Fire Giants. Upon learning that the Flame of Ruin would never die, however, she cursed the last of the giants to tend to it for eternity. Marika declared the path to the mountains as Forbidden Lands and excised details of the war from history.
Marika and Godfrey would rule together and have at least three children: Godwyn the Golden and the twins Morgott and Mohg. The twins were both born as accursed Omen and were hidden in the depths below the capital. Even so, Godfrey and his Golden Lineage would be counted as the first of the demigods. Godfrey went on to conquer many lands for the Erdtree.
At some point during Marika's rule, the Gloam-Eyed Queen and her Godskin Apostles wielded the power of god-slaying black flame to carry out a god hunt. Marika thus desired to seal the Rune of Death 's power. Her Shadowbound Beast and half-brother Maliketh defeated the Gloam-Eyed Queen and her apostles, and then she extracted the Rune of Death from the Elden Ring. Maliketh sealed it in his black blade, founding the Golden Order and establishing Marika as the one true god.
When the Erdtree sensed its mortality for the first time and released its Golden Seeds to spawn the Minor Erdtrees, revealing itself not as perfect and eternal as those who worshipped it believed it to be, Marika would abandon blind faith and declare at the Minor Erdtree Church her intent to inquire into the depths of her Golden Order in an attempt to strengthen its grace through understanding.
Queen Marika punished the cutting-gifted tribe for discovering the nature of her original sin.

#### Liurnian Wars

Queen Marika desired to conquer Liurnia of the Lakes, the land of the Academy of Raya Lucaria and the Carian Royal Family. She declared war on the glintstone sorcerers and sent a golden host led by her secret other half: a red-haired champion named Radagon. The champions of the moon, led by Rennala, repelled the glorious warriors of the Erdtree for a time, but it ended in a draw. In the second Liurnian War, Radagon invades again, but this time, he uses the Celestial Dew at the Church of Vows in a ritual to recant his aggressions and seek absolution. The Carian Queen accepted, and their wrongdoings were washed away. The two were wed and produced three children: Radahn, Rykard, and Ranni.

#### Crusade

Marika sent her son Messmer away on a crusade into the lands of the Hornsent. Despite her precautions, her fear of the Abyssal Serpent sealed within him led her to trap him within the Realm of Shadow, where he would wage war to exterminate the Hornsent and devastate their lands. No matter how many of her enemies burned in his flame, however, Messmer received no sign from Marika to end his crusade. Marika kept these events and the land of shadow hidden away and removed from history.

#### Godfrey's Banishment

With her rule secure, Marika announced her intention to search the depths of the Golden Order, declaring the early days of blind belief a thing long past. When Godfrey's last foe fell, it was said the hue of his eyes had faded. Marika banished him and his kinfolk from the Lands Between, robbing them of their grace, thus creating the first Tarnished, all in the hope that they would grow stronger by waging war in lands outside her rule, dying, and then eventually being revived with their guidance of grace returned.

### King Consort Radagon

After Godfrey's banishment from the Lands Between, Marika took Radagon as her second husband and King Consort, elevating him to become the second Elden Lord. Radagon already had three children with Queen Rennala of the Carian Royal Family, and after his union with Marika, she would raise her new step-children, Radahn, Rykard, and Ranni to demigod status.
Marika and Radagon would go on to have two more children of their own, the twins Malenia and Miquella, who like Marika herself and Radagon's daughter Ranni, were Empyreans, and were thus potential successors to Marika. However, Radagon and Marika were in truth the same. Radagon was described as Marika's male "other self". Thus, the twins were the children of a single god, and they were both born afflicted: Malenia with rotting sickness and Miquella with eternal childhood.

### Night of Black Knives

On the Night of Black Knives, a fragment of the Rune of Death was stolen from Maliketh. For a long time, it was not known who was behind the plot, but it is finally revealed it was in fact Ranni who stole the fragment of the rune to forge god-slaying black knives through a fearsome rite.
The Black Knife Assassins, Numen women purported to have close ties to Marika herself, used these knives to slay Godwyn the Golden, the first recorded death of a demigod in all history. Queen Marika is said to have been driven to the brink by the death of Godwyn, and she instructed her Demigod children to fight for the positions of Elden Lord and that of godhood.

### The Shattering

Some time after the Night of Black Knives, Queen Marika used her Numen hammer to shatter the Elden Ring. Fragments of the ring splintered into Great Runes and spread across the Lands Between, starting a conflict between her demigod children. War after war was fought, but there was no victor. For her part, Marika would be imprisoned inside the Erdtree, possibly by the Elden Beast or the Greater Will itself.
Marika's trespass demanded a heavy sentence. But even in shackles, she remains a god, and the vision's vessel. - Two Fingers — Finger Reader Enia

### Game Events

True to her promise to Godfrey and his warriors, grace was restored to the banished Tarnished. They would rise from their graves and journey to the Lands Between to once again brandish the Elden Ring. The Tarnished formed the Roundtable Hold, where they worked to realize the goal Marika had given them. They would be served by a Misbegotten blacksmith named Hewg, whom Marika herself had charged with smithing a "god-slaying weapon" before his imprisonment in the Hold.
Eventually, the Tarnished manages to fight their way into the Erdtree, where the shattered Marika lies imprisoned. Marika is suddenly released from her bonds, as Radagon asserts control over their shared body and confronts the Tarnished. After defeating Radagon and the Elden Beast itself, the Tarnished may choose what to do with the fractured body of Marika. Marika's head is restored to her frame if the Elden Ring is mended, either by itself or with a Mending Rune. If Ranni is summoned, she picks up Marika's head gently, peacefully dissipating along with the rest of her body. If the Tarnished is the Lord of Frenzied Flame, Marika's head breaks apart and crumbles into ash.
By choosing to go down the Elden Lord path, whether the regular one or using any of the three Mending Runes to fix the Elden Ring within Marika, the Tarnished champion ushers in a new age. They become the third Elden Lord and effectively Marika's new consort in every one of them, except in the Age of the Stars (where they become consort to Ranni) and in the Lord of Frenzied Flame ending (where they make the whole world burn). However, Queen Marika remains shattered and unresponsive, with only the Elden Ring itself shining brightly within her body.

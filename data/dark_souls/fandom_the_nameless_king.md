# The Nameless King
## Location

Found in Archdragon Peak, summoned by ringing the Great Belfry.

## Lore

In-Game Description

### Dark Souls

The Nameless King is the firstborn child of Gwyn, Lord of Sunlight and one of the deities who resided in Anor Londo during the Age of Fire. He was worshipped by humans as the " god of war ".
During the war against the Dragons, the eldest son took part in the fighting together with Dragon Slayer Ornstein and his father Gwyn, using sunlight like him to create lightning spears and infuse his weapons to damage the creatures' scales. The god of war distinguished himself as a dragon slayer, using his swordspear imbued with lightning and even creating his personal armor with the scales of his preys. In addition, Ornstein, as his companion during the war, became his first knight and as a god of war, the firstborn created his own covenant of the Warrior of Sunlight, a group of knights who venerated the heir of the sun and supported other fighters in battle, obtaining medals with the symbol of the divinity as trophies. The eldest would recognize their efforts and reward them by teaching them his lightning miracles. The sun warriors would also develop an original miracle based on the story of the Nameless King, Ornstein, and the brave dragonslayers in their service.
After his father Gwyn left to link the First Flame, the throne of Anor Londo would pass to the eldest son of the King; and so the Nameless King inherited the Sunlight of his father. He would thus wear the golden crown, and succeed his father's titles of "king of the gods" and "god of the sun". The Nameless King was said to be inferior to none in terms of military strength. Gwyn's firstborn preserved his father's design and instructions, shepherding humans on the path to the Linking of the First Flame to continue the Age of Fire. In fact, underneath Anor Londo, a city was built exclusively for the undead, with an altar inside for the firstborn of Gwyn, and the aim of guiding a chosen undead who would one day sacrifice themselves to the Flame. Inside the city ​​was the Undead Parish, a church at the center of the cult of the burg and connected to the first test to start on the path of Firelinking and ring the first Bell of Awakening. Furthermore, the town was connected to the Firelink Shrine where Kingseeker Frampt awaited the champion and directed them on their journey.
However, the rest of the firstborn's reign was marked by catastrophes. The Four Kings of New Londo were corrupted by the dark power of Darkstalker Kaathe, starting to sow devastation nearby the city of the gods. Following Knight Artorias 's failure to defeat the Darkwraiths, the gods were forced to flood the city, sacrificing all the humans within, and seal it. A similar event happened in Oolacile when, again due to Kaathe, the awakening of the primordial human Manus and his fury led to the collapse of the country when Artorias was unable to defeat him. In addition to the grave situation on the borders of Anor Londo, the city of the gods itself became a dangerous place when a group of cultists began targeting the clan of Gwyn and their kind, and occult weapons capable of killing the gods reached inside the walls of Anor Londo. Furthermore, in the city of the gods Seath the Scaleless had now completely lost his mind, transforming his Archives into a prison and going so far as to kidnap the maidens of Gwynevere to carry out experiments on them.
The eldest son of Gwyn eventually decided to "sacrifice everything" to ally himself with the dragons. In the eyes of the other deities of Anor Londo, archenemies of the dragons, his actions amounted to an unforgivable sin; he was therefore dethroned and exiled from the city. The former king was deprived of his deific status, his statues destroyed and his name erased from the annals of history. Before leaving Anor Londo, the eldest left his sunlight blade miracle and ring on Gwyn's empty tomb in his mausoleum as a final farewell, still paying respects for his father. Now excommunicated from the pantheon of the gods and without a home, the god of war continued to watch closely over his warriors of the sunlight, listening to their prayers and rewarding the proofs of their exploits with his miracles of Sunlight Spear and Great Lightning Spear.

### Dark Souls II

At some point the followers of the god of the sun reached Drangleic. In the Harvest Valley, a region under the control of Alken, the remains of an altar that venerated the Nameless King can be found in the form of an exact replica of his ancient statues, implying that the god of the sun was worshipped until the fall of the kingdom.
The ancient Warriors of Sunlight, as of the time of Drangleic, are either no longer extant or lack the desire to make their presence known. Nevertheless, their very absence has made the tales of their brave deeds all the more alluring. Solaire of Astora 's deeds in particular have been memorialized and his artifacts passed down in the cult of the Heirs of the Sun, who continue to act to help other warriors in times of need, following the original philosophy of the cult of the sun and collecting evidence of their successes in the form of ancient medals with the symbol of the sun to honor the most worthy servants of the sun god.
The Falconers, a group of mercenaries hired to protect the elites of Volgen, were still part of the covenant of those that worshipped the God of the Sun and possessed their Sunlight Medals. Moreover, in Lindelt, a "sacred land" known for its clerics and miracles, the Nameless King's miracles of Lightning Spear and Great Lightning Spear were preserved and passed down within its religion, although the names of the clan of gods and the sun god had been lost. Furthermore, some other miracles of the god such as the Sunlight Blade and the Sacred Oath appear to have been part of the lost culture of Olaphis, indicating a continuing tradition of the cult of the Nameless King, even if fragmentary, throughout the history of Drangleic.

### Dark Souls III

Over the centuries, the Nameless King would continue to lead a warrior's life and take part in countless battles, fighting alongside his stormdrake with whom he forged a relationship of profound camaraderie, coming to consider him as a comrade in arms as was an ancient custom among soldiers of the gods.
In the heyday of Lothric, when the descendants of Drangleic and followers of the Way of Blue and the Heirs of the Sun moved to another location, they still remembered the Nameless King as their God of the Sun. As the early inhabitants of Lothric found themselves facing large numbers of wyverns, they had to become dragonslayers to survive; and so they turned to the Nameless King, building an altar for him. The first knights of Lothric devoted themselves to the worship of the ancestral deity of the sun and war, who in return gave them the tools to face the wyverns. Their first armors were heavy masses of iron capable of withstanding dragon fire and similar in design to the ancient armor of the dragonslayers of Anor Londo, while their shields were capable of absorbing the fierce lightning attacks of wyverns. From the sun god they also soon learned how to infuse their weapons with the power of lightning, lethal against dragons, and the ancient techniques for hunting archdragons. Finally they learned to use his miracles which allowed them to manipulate the power of lightning. The cult of the Nameless King remained prevalent among the soldiery of Lothric until the last days of the kingdom, as one of his altars can be found right inside the military barracks, and the knights of the kingdom seem to still be part of the Warriors of Sunlight covenant.

### Theories

- It is possible to theorize that the name of Gwyn's firstborn was "Gwyn- Sen " (セン). Before linking the First Flame, Lord Gwyn had entrusted his "male children" with the task of shepherding humans into following his example, through the prophecy of the Chosen Undead. The Undead Burg was likely built under the reign of the firstborn, as a statue of him can be found near the Undead Parish. Consequently, it is possible to theorize that Sen's Fortress, created as a proving ground to access Anor Londo and obtain the Lordvessel, all steps required as proofs of the prophecy, was also created by the firstborn, thereby making its name "Sen's Fortress"(センの古城). The particle "Gwyn" before the name comes from the fact that Gwyn's other children, known and openly recognized as legitimate, carry it in their name like "Gwyn-evere" and "Gwyn-dolin".
- It is possible to theorize that Faraam is the name that the people of Forossa used in veneration of Gwyn's firstborn after his name was lost over the centuries. Not only does Faraam share with the Nameless King the title of "god of war", but the god was also able to bless the armor of Forossa Knights, whose helmet depicted a knight fighting an enormous dragon, implying that Faraam was not a "false" deity created by the legends of men but an active entity in the kingdom who was capable of performing the miracles of the ancient gods. The Lion Knights of Forossa, besides bearing the title "Lion" same as Gwyn's firstborn and first knight, were not only mighty but rabid warriors obsessed with fighting; Forossan knights knew only war and no other ways of living, to the point that they didn't even know what they were fighting for, similarly to the Nameless King who "had respect only for arms, and nothing else".

## Strategy


### Phase 1: King of the Storm

The fight begins with the Nameless King entering on his mount from the midway point of the arena. He then proceeds to attack the player through either swipes and slams from his spear. Lightning bolts during flight and flames fired from his mount. Both have two qualifiers; one being that he touches down nearby the player and sprays a swatch of fire in their direction, to cover a large area. The other being when the player stays under the mounts body and is reminiscent of the Ancient Dragons attack of flying straight up and flooding the area under him with a long burst of fire. This can easily kill the player as it will stun them in the first hit, knocking them down and, hit them once again as they recover.
All attacks should either be leveled at the mounts head, or the rider himself. Several attacks are enough to stagger the mount long enough for a critical, or if one has sufficient int/faith. A spell can be used to possibly once shot the rider. A Sunlight Spear and standard charm were enough to accomplish this task on NG+1.

### Phase 2: The Nameless King

The Nameless King will dismount and absorb the strength of his mount after it is defeated. His attacks will be dragged out when going after the player. Usually catching an unprepared fighter off guard as they roll too early and are struck for heavy damage. Which on later a play through, can nearly one hit the player in several instances. His attacks will either be followed with large burst of wind, or lightning and he also possesses several attacks that can release ranged bolts on the player. Along with a grab that can travel up to 20 units in the player's direction.
It is advised that the player wait for him to finish his attacks before attacking him during his recovery from the combo, or strike him when he is stuck telegraphing his more powerful maneuvers.
